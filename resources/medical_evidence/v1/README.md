# Medical evidence resource bundle v1

This directory contains the inherited runtime subset of the original
`medical_consistency/disease_evidence/output/disease_map_full_batch` bundle.
It is used to map endpoint-specific medical evidence to model features.
It also includes unscored reference annotations for selected template statements.

Included resource files:

- `risk_factor_evidence.csv`
- `decision_screening_evidence.csv`
- `consensus_evidence.csv`
- `disease_evidence_feature_map.csv`
- `disease_master.csv`
- `compressed_disease_resolution.csv`
- `pipeline_summary.json`
- `disease_metadata/outcome_filter.csv`
- `disease_metadata/outcome_test.csv`
- `disease_metadata/disease_map_full.csv`
- `SHA256SUMS`
- `translation_manifest.json`
- `source_annotations.csv` (reference supplement; not loaded by the scorer)

The three disease metadata CSVs originate from the original
`medical_consistency/disease_evidence` directory. They restore endpoint
NAME/LONGNAME and compressed-name matching. The English-only distribution
translates the headings, categories and review notes in `disease_map_full.csv`;
the outcome filter/test files retain their original bytes. The scorer discovers them under
`disease_metadata/`; `consistency.disease_metadata_dir` can select another
directory. `SHA256SUMS` records the distributed bytes. Original and translated
hashes are recorded separately in `translation_manifest.json`; historical source
hashes are retained in the selected scientific subset
`docs/provenance/source_fingerprints.json`.

## Statement provenance and scoring limitations

All 852 risk-factor rows and 852 decision/screening rows (1,704 statements)
were generated by disease-name and disease-group templates in the original
`compressed_batch_pipeline.py`, rather than extracted from identified guideline
documents or publications. Their `candidate_id`/`document_id` values identify
seeds, `document_title` is `Template-generated seed evidence`, and
`review_status=seed_template`. Every statement retains `needs_manual_review=1`
and `certainty_imputed=1`. The historical `source_url`, `guideline_year` and
`last_reviewed` fields are empty; no original document attribution or human
medical review has been established for these statements.

The institution names in `source` and `source_family`, and the
`evidence_type=guideline` label, were assigned by the template pipeline. They
are not evidence that the named institution published or endorsed the wording.
The `certainty`, `strength` and rank fields are heuristic/imputed labels,
not grades extracted from a verified guideline or assessed by a clinician.
These values remain frozen for reproducibility. Four appended fields expose
this distinction on every statement: `statement_origin`,
`source_attribution_status`, `provenance_note` and `reference_annotation_ids`.
All original columns, statements, scoring values and row order are retained.

`consensus_evidence.csv` also comes from these templates. Each of its 1,704 rows
has `source_count=1` and `statement_count=1`; the name does not imply agreement
among independent sources or experts. The current scorer does not exclude
pending-review statements: it discounts their weight and reliability. Inherited
consensus rows omit the review/imputation flags and do not receive those same
flag-based discounts. Source attribution and medical validity are not checked
by the scoring algorithm.

Accordingly, a consistency score measures alignment with this template-derived
rule set and its feature mappings. A high score is not evidence of clinical
correctness, current guideline adherence, causal validity, calibrated clinical
risk or safe patient-level decisions; a low score is not proof of a medical
error. Software regression tests establish reproducible matching and scoring,
not the clinical validity of the statements. Claims about medical consistency
require source-derived, endpoint-appropriate rules and independent medical
review and validation.

## Checked reference supplement

`source_annotations.csv` contains 129 source-backed paraphrases from 64 distinct
official source documents, including 60 guidelines or screening recommendations,
checked on 2026-10-10. They are linked to 89 seed statements across 63 disease
entries; the other 1,615 seed statements have no checked reference annotation.
This is selected coverage, not a systematic or exhaustive medical
evidence review. All 1,704 original statements remain unverified as to their
original source and pending medical review, including those with annotations.

Each annotation identifies a reference title, organization, URL, year and year
basis, recommendation/section locator, supported statement, relationship to
the seed and applicable scope. Records may include structured `reference_population`, `reference_threshold`
and `reference_version_status` fields. Blank values mean that a structured field
is not supplied; the record's existing scope and locator still apply. `reference_document_type`
distinguishes guidelines or screening recommendations, evidence summaries and
official health information. A PDQ evidence summary is not an NCI/NIH policy
statement; NEI health information is not a numbered clinical guideline. Source
document counts and guideline counts are recorded separately; different ADA
2026 chapters belong to one annual guideline.

`supports_specific_component` supports only the
specified component; `refines_or_replaces_seed` supplies a more precise
alternative or identifies a template mismatch. `additional_management_context`
covers treatment, referral or post-diagnosis assessment; it does not substantiate
the linked seed's diagnostic or risk-factor claim. None of these relationships
validates the entire seed. For example, the supplement distinguishes obesity from the seed's
BMI 25 threshold, dementia assessment from its focal-neurology template, and
acute kidney injury assessment from generic CKD testing. References are not
automatically propagated to related disease subtypes or synonyms.

Current-source and historical-source checks are distinguished in
`reference_version_status`. The checked GOLD and GINA 2025 editions are
historical references with available 2026 successors. KDIGO entries identify
the original 2024 edition without claiming a latest-version check. Numeric
cutoffs remain conditional on their stated population and clinical pathway;
an insufficient-evidence statement is not a recommendation against screening.

Population boundaries, numeric thresholds and source versions delimit the
meaning of each annotation. A referral cutoff is not a diagnostic cutoff,
and a subgroup guideline does not apply to every disease subtype. The
`reference_access_basis` value `official_document_direct_fetch` denotes a
source obtained from its official publisher or repository;
`official_document_web_extract` denotes inspected publisher text. Neither
value establishes human clinical review.

`reference_checked_on` records document checking, not human clinical review;
`reference_year` uses the explicitly stated publication or update basis and
can differ from the year attached to an individual recommendation.
Every annotation has `verification_status=document_checked_clinical_review_pending`,
`needs_manual_review=1` and an empty `clinical_review_date`. The historical
seed citation and review fields remain empty because a newly located reference
does not reconstruct the original seed provenance. Full source documents and
intermediate extraction records are not distributed in this bundle.

The scorer does not read this supplement or the four new statement metadata
fields. Compiled evidence weights, reliability and rule arrays are unchanged;
the supplement does not improve or clinically validate current scores. Resource
bytes and hashes have changed, and `SHA256SUMS` records the new distributed
identities. Historical source fingerprints and the language-only translation
manifest remain intact. Replaying a frozen artifact still requires its original
resource bundle; do not substitute the new hashes into a historical artifact.

Category labels and administrative notes in `disease_master.csv` and
`compressed_disease_resolution.csv` are also translated to English, with the
category heading named `disease_category`. Disease identifiers, model labels,
ICD codes, English disease aliases, rule weights and row order are preserved.
The language conversion is verified against the original endpoint lookup and
compiled evidence arrays; it does not constitute a new medical review.

Generation scripts, review queues, intermediate candidates, API credentials,
and LLM request/response files are deliberately not part of the runtime
package. Selected scientific source fingerprints and their scope are documented
in `docs/provenance/README.md`; a complete private source inventory is not distributed.
